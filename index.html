About

I am a neuroscientist interested in understanding the biological mechanisms underlying Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders. My research training has included experimental models of neurodegeneration, molecular and cellular neuroscience, and increasingly computational analysis of complex biological datasets.

Before entering neuroscience, I practiced law. My transition into biomedical research was shaped in part by my family's experience with Alzheimer's disease. That background continues to influence my interest in translational research, science policy, and communicating the value of neuroscience beyond academia.

Research

My long-term goal is to understand why neurodegenerative diseases develop, identify mechanisms that influence disease progression, and help translate biological discoveries into better tools for diagnosis and treatment.

Neurodegeneration

Investigating molecular and cellular mechanisms that contribute to neuronal vulnerability and disease progression.

Human Data & Multi-omics

Integrating transcriptomic, genomic, and other large-scale datasets to identify disease-associated pathways and cell states.

Computational Translation

Developing computational skills to connect experimental findings with human disease biology, biomarkers, and therapeutic discovery.
