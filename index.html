About

I am Jung Hyun Park, or JH, a neuroscientist studying Alzheimer's disease and neurodegeneration. I completed my PhD in Neuroscience at Indiana University, where I investigated interactions between amyloid-β and tau pathology using Alzheimer's disease mouse models and single-cell transcriptomic approaches. My work has focused particularly on glial responses and lipid dysregulation, with cross-species comparison to human Alzheimer's disease datasets.

My path to neuroscience has been unconventional. Before beginning my scientific training, I was an attorney and patent attorney in South Korea and later served as Director of Legal Affairs for the PyeongChang 2018 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games. My family's experience with dementia ultimately led me to change careers and pursue neuroscience.

I am now interested in moving increasingly toward computational and data-driven approaches to neurodegenerative disease, while drawing on my experimental background to connect mechanisms observed in disease models with human disease biology.

Research

Aβ-Tau Co-pathology

My PhD research examined how combined amyloid and tau pathology alters cellular and molecular responses beyond those observed with either pathology alone in Alzheimer's disease mouse models.

Glial Biology & Lipid Dysregulation

I use transcriptomic approaches to investigate disease-associated responses in microglia, oligodendrocytes, and other glial populations, with particular interest in lipid metabolism and immune pathways.

Mouse-to-Human Translation

I compare molecular signatures identified in experimental models with human Alzheimer's disease datasets to ask which disease-associated programs are shared across species and where model systems diverge.

Current Direction

I am expanding my work toward human datasets, multi-omic analysis, and computational approaches that can help bridge mechanistic studies in experimental models with translational questions in Alzheimer's disease.
